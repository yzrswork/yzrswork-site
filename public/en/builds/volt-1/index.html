How I turned a broken digital watch into a tiny 5V voltmeter

volt-1 reuses the circular silver case of a broken ALBA SPOON digital watch as the visible bezel for a small green DC voltmeter. It no longer tells time: it displays the voltage of the supply powering it. A tool that measured time became a tool that measures voltage.

By YZRS WORK · Original build article: April 24, 2026 · English adaptation: October 7, 2026

What it does / who it is for

This is a small supply-voltage display and a salvage build for makers who want to reuse an interesting enclosure. It is not a restored watch or a calibrated handheld tester. The useful pattern is keeping a donor object's opening and building an insulating mount behind it; you do not need this exact Japanese watch to reproduce that idea.

Tested status at a glance

  • Original build: powered from 5 V USB; the owner reported approximately 5.1X on the display. Initial functional confirmation only.
  • October 4, 2026: the production exhibition configuration including volt-1 ran approximately 2–3 hours with no abnormality recorded. Cold start, voltage / voltage-drop, mobile-battery auto-off, and backup-supply checks passed.
  • Limits: practical exhibition acceptance, not laboratory qualification. Calibration accuracy, standalone current draw, efficiency, startup surge, and protection thresholds remain unknown. Detailed numeric voltage readings were not preserved for the October acceptance.

Why keep the broken watch?

The donor was an ALBA SPOON digital watch, recorded as W620-4140. Its LCD had missing segments. I took it apart and attempted a repair, then abandoned restoring the watch function. The published account describes the difficulty and parts-sourcing burden; it does not establish an exact technical reason that repair was impossible.

The circular opening and silver case were still worth keeping. I had a small voltmeter on hand, so the question changed from “Can I repair this watch?” to “Can this opening become a useful display again?” The watch's physical identity stayed; its job changed.

Required tools

  • Small screwdrivers and suitable tools for disassembling your donor object.
  • Acrylic scoring cutter, cutting mat, and layout tools for the mount plate.
  • Drill press, suitable metal drill bits, secure clamping, and eye protection for small metal parts.
  • Tools for the selected M2 screws and spacers, wire cutters / pliers, and a hot-glue tool.
  • A means to verify supply polarity before powering. A separate suitable meter is useful for your own checks; no comparison-meter accuracy test is documented for this build.

Build steps / design decisions

This is the recorded sequence with reproduction checks added. Layout and dimensions must follow your own donor; there is no universal drilling template.

Prepare the salvaged case

The watch was disassembled and the circular case retained. For reproduction, remove the watch battery and unnecessary original electronics before any drilling. Inspect the empty case and plan the opening, plate, meter position, and wire route before cutting.

Make the insulating mounting plate

I cut 2 mm black acrylic to replace the glass plate in the small silver frame. The published method used an acrylic cutter to score and snap the sheet. Use a cutting mat: the original build account also records a cutter slipping and marking the desk. The black plate provided the mount behind the silver case.

Position and retain the voltmeter

I used M2 screws to retain the mini voltmeter on the acrylic, adjusted spacers until the digits could be read through the watch opening, and added hot-glue reinforcement. Check position and rear clearance before committing to the mount. The source does not preserve a transferable spacer height or a verified mounting-hole pattern.

Use the existing lug area for case retention

I drilled through the watch's existing lug area and passed 0.6 mm copper wire through it to retain the case on the acrylic. This reused the watch's attachment area instead of treating the case as a glued-on decoration. The wire serves a mechanical role here.

Failure: the small drill bit broke

The 1 mm bit broke during the work. I switched to a 2 mm bit and finished the holes, but I did not isolate whether the failure came from bit size, technique, material, or a combination. A successful continuation does not prove that 2 mm is the right size for every donor. Clamp small metal parts securely and wear eye protection; thin bits can break.

Wiring / connection

The meter is two-wire and self-powered: it displays the voltage of the DC supply connected to it. For reproduction, the connection is 5 V DC source → voltmeter. Identify the selected meter's positive and negative connections from its own documentation and verify polarity before power-up. No source-confirmed connector pinout or wire-color polarity diagram is provided here.

The current exhibition path is PDB-1 5V/GND → SPL-2 → volt-1. It uses the exhibition's 5V MAIN supply, with exposed connections requiring short prevention and insulation where necessary. PDB-1 and SPL-2 describe this configuration; neither is a reproduction requirement.

Failure / troubleshooting boundaries

  • Missing watch segments: repair was attempted and abandoned. The exact failure mechanism remains unknown; this page does not diagnose watch LCD faults.
  • Broken drill bit: the recorded recovery was switching from 1 mm to 2 mm. The cause was not isolated.
  • Reproduction checks: if your chosen meter does not start or cannot be read, disconnect power and check documented range, polarity, connections, and opening alignment. These are editorial checks, not additional failures observed on volt-1.

Alternatives / substitutions

A discarded instrument bezel or another small obsolete enclosure is a possible starting point if it meets PI01. A different insulating sheet or fastening method may work if stiffness, clearances, and retention suit the selected parts. These are design options, not YZRS-tested substitute builds; physical fit is not verified.

For US sourcing, first obtain the meter documentation and dimensions, then compare the donor opening and rear space. A generic mini-meter listing without a confirmed two-wire topology and supported 5 V operation is not enough. No US candidate was adequately verified for this implementation, so no SKU is listed.

Safety / limits / do not do

This article's scope is 5 V DC only. Do not connect this build directly to mains or AC. Verify the selected voltmeter's supported voltage range and do not exceed it. Confirm polarity before powering; disconnect power during wiring and mechanical work. Remove the donor watch battery and unnecessary original electronics before drilling. Clamp small metal parts securely and wear eye protection; thin drill bits can break. Avoid shorts between exposed 5 V and GND, and insulate exposed conductors where necessary.

These are reproduction precautions added for readers, not proof that each precaution was documented in the original build. Passing exhibition checks does not establish calibration, protective behavior, certification, or suitability for other electrical systems.
